# Landing copy

Good page copy says what the product does, for whom and why to believe it, in the words the customer already uses. The inputs decide most of it; the writing only makes it clear.

## Inputs (read before writing)

1. `.agents/product-context.md`: ideal customer, positioning, objections, proof and customer language. If it is missing, use the `product-context` skill first or ask the owner.
2. The keyword map for the page: its single search intent, main query and the questions the page must answer.
3. `.agents/voice.md` if it exists.
4. The page's goal: the one action a visitor should take.

If a required input is missing, say which one and stop rather than filling the gap with guesses.

## Structure

Write in this order, one section at a time:

| Section | What it does | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | Says what the product does and for whom, or the result the customer gets | Could it sit on a competitor's page unchanged? Then rewrite it |
| Subheadline | Adds how it works or the main differentiator | One or two sentences, no new promises |
| Primary CTA | The single action, in the visitor's terms ("See the plans", not "Submit") | Matches the page goal |
| Benefits | Three to five, each an outcome followed by the feature that delivers it | Each one has proof or a concrete detail |
| Proof | Approved numbers, testimonials, client names, demos, screenshots | Every item marked approved in product-context |
| Objections | The top objections with honest answers | Answers come from product-context, not invented |
| Questions | Answers to the keyword map's questions, direct answer first | Matches the page's intent |
| Final CTA | Repeats the action with the main reason to take it now | No fake urgency |

## Variants

Give two or three variants for the headline, subheadline and primary CTA, each built on a different angle (outcome, pain, differentiator) and labeled with it, so the owner can pick or test them. Recommend one and say why.

## Rules

- Every claim needs proof from product-context or the owner. If there is none, soften the claim to what is true or leave a marked placeholder: `[PROOF NEEDED: ...]`.
- Never invent numbers, percentages, testimonials, quotes, client names, logos, awards or "trusted by" counts. A placeholder is better than a fabrication.
- No fake scarcity or urgency (countdowns, "only 3 left") unless it is real and the owner confirms it.
- Use the customer's words from product-context over marketing words. Avoid empty phrases ("unlock", "transform", "next level", "seamless").
- Respect the keyword map: the main query appears naturally in the headline or subheadline and in the title; no keyword stuffing.
- Write in the language of the page's audience, and follow `voice.md`.

## Output

Copy per section with variants, then a list of every claim with its source in product-context, and a list of placeholders and open questions for the owner.
